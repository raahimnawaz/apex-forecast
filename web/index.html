      What this model does and does not know

      • It cannot separate driver from car. Every team fields exactly two cars, so within a single race the team effect is definitionally the mean of its two drivers. These numbers are driver-in-car pace. Splitting them into constructor advantage and driver skill needs variation across team-mate pairings and is the next layer's job, not this one's.
      • Fuel burn and track evolution are not separately identifiable from race laps alone — both are smooth functions of lap number. The model carries one combined trend term and reports it as such rather than assuming a textbook kg/lap split.
      • Ten races of in-regulation data. 2026 reset the power units, the aerodynamics and the car dimensions, so pre-2026 pace does not transfer. The intervals here are wide because the evidence is genuinely thin, and they should not be narrowed by borrowing from 2025.
      • Cadillac has no history at all. Its estimates rest entirely on ten races as a debut team and deserve more scepticism than the rest of the grid.
      • Known bias: backmarkers spend more time in traffic and lift for blue flags. The dirty-air term absorbs some of that, but not all, so the tail of the field is likely flattered less than it deserves.
      • Compound ordering is not yet resolved: medium and soft degradation differ by less than their spread across races, so this data cannot rank them. Soft runs are short, which limits the tyre-age range the slope is fitted over.
      • Fit quality: Monaco is the weakest fit of the season, which is the expected place for a pace model to struggle — the race is traffic, not pace.
      • Why team colours never encode a value here. Run through a colour-vision validator, the 2026 constructor palette fails outright on the dark surface: Haas silver and Mercedes teal are ΔE 0.6 apart under deuteranopia — effectively the same colour — and Alpine against Red Bull is ΔE 9.2 apart under normal vision, below the legibility floor. So team colour is a chip beside a text driver code and nothing more; every quantitative scale on this page uses a validated ramp instead.
