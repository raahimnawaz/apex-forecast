            What this model does not know

            • Ten races of in-regulation data. 2026 reset the power units, aerodynamics and car dimensions, so pre-2026 pace does not transfer. Wide intervals are thin evidence, not hedging.
            • Retirements are drawn independently — and that turns out to be fine. The obvious objection is that real races retire cars in clumps. Tested: 6 of 41 timed retirements fall within a lap of another, against 5.5 expected by chance (sd 3.0), p = 0.52. There is no clustering to model, so the independent draw stands and the correlated version was dropped.
            • Safety cars are reduced-form. A safety car raises the entropy of the finishing order rather than being simulated lap by lap through the pit lane. It is a stand-in for the mechanism, not the mechanism — and now that a strategy layer exists, the sharpest thing it misses is that a safety car is a half-price pit stop.
            • Pit strategy is built and does not ship. Each car's minimum-time stop plan is computed from its own measured degradation and the circuit's measured pit loss. Scored out of sample it forecast no better than leaving it out — RPS 0.1028 against 0.1026 — so it stays a calibration variant rather than part of the forecast.
            • The tail is thin. Win probabilities from the back round below 0.1%. The safety-car process gives a midfield car some route to the front, but it is a reduced-form one. Treat anything under ~1% as "no opinion".
            • Team form is circuit-dependent and the model does not know it. A team's corrected pace varies by circuit far beyond noise (p = 5e-07; adjusted R² 0.860 → 0.923). It is the largest unexploited signal here and it is not yet forecastable: every circuit is raced once a season, so each team-circuit pair has one race of evidence and nothing carries across the regulation reset.
            • The driver/car split is weakly identified. Each team runs two cars, so adding a constant to both team-mates and subtracting it from their car changes nothing. Only drivers moving between teams break the tie — read the intervals, not the point estimates.
            • Cadillac has no history. A debut team judged on ten races; its interval is the widest on the grid for good reason.
            • Backmarkers are flattered less than they deserve. They spend more time in traffic and lift for blue flags; the dirty-air term absorbs some of that, not all.
            • Soft and medium degradation cannot be separated — they differ by less than their spread across races.
